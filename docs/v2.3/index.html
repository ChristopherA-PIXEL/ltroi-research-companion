What costs less to keep on the road?

Stress-test the synthetic two-cohort illustration from the LTROI v2.3 working paper. Change the Weibull-style recurrent-wear parameters, repair-parts expenditure and real discount rate; see how the equivalent annual service-continuity cost changes.

Interpretation boundary: These are hypothetical assets, not UK makes or models. Outputs include acquisition, replacement and terminal resale, so this is a restricted TCO-extension EAC scenario, not empirical model LTROI, a complete vehicle TCO estimate, or a reliability ranking. Fuel, insurance, collision losses and warranty reimbursements are excluded.

Research and reproduction

The scientific release should permit public scrutiny of the core reproducibility script independently of a marketing opt-in. Optional lead capture for supplementary practitioner materials is a separate, consent-based workflow.

Source: Aaron Christopher, Discounted Automotive Ownership Risk: An Actuarial–Economic Framework (LTROI v2.3), research-circulation edition, October 2026. This demonstration uses the published synthetic parameters. No empirical model rankings are inferred.

Limitations: Stationary fixed charges, parameterised minimal repairs, simplified resale curve and a constrained year-eight fault decision. No stochastic severity distributions, observed repair invoices, cohort denominators or model validation.

Synthetic-only research companion. No third-party warranty data, analytics, email capture, or transmitted feedback is included. These illustrative calculations are not car purchasing advice. The frozen LTROI v2.3 research archive remains unchanged.
